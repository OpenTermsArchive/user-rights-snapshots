Technology plays an important role in enabling people to exercise their human rights. With a global community of more than one billion people, TikTok is committed to respecting the human rights of everyone impacted by our platform and ensuring they are treated with dignity and respect.

Our commitment to human rights

Respecting human rights is a shared responsibility. At TikTok, it is fundamental to building and maintaining the trust of our employees, users, creators, advertisers, and broader community. Our approach is guided by internationally recognized human rights standards and frameworks, including:

  • The International Bill of Human Rights, including the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR), the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR), and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)
  • The International Labour Organization (ILO) Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work
  • The Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC)
  • The United Nations Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs)


As part of our commitment, we strive to respect human rights across our operations and business relationships, while complying with applicable laws and regulations that promote and protect human rights globally. We endeavor to continuously evaluate our operations to identify, assess, and address salient human rights risks, engage key rights holders, and prioritize areas where we have the greatest opportunity to have a positive impact. Key leaders across TikTok provide oversight of human rights-related actions and decisions, supported by a cross-functional working group that coordinates and advances our human rights initiatives.

As a global company, we are required to comply with local laws in the countries where we operate. In some cases, these laws may conflict with international human rights standards. When this occurs, we remain committed to respecting human rights and protecting our users’ rights to free expression and privacy.

To do so, we assess the validity of government requests that may conflict with these principles and interpret them as narrowly as possible. Where we believe this outcome does not protect users’ rights, we may pursue available legal avenues to challenge requests that could undermine international law or human rights standards.

Key priorities

As a global platform, serving more than a billion people globally, we routinely assess our operations to help ensure we are upholding human rights. Below, we set out some of the key areas that we prioritise as part of this commitment.

Human rights risk identification & mitigation

We strive to proactively identify and mitigate human rights risks across our business. Some actions include:

  • Training and capacity building: Periodic trainings are provided for our employees, which cover topics related to human rights.
  • Product development and deployment: Safety, privacy, and legal reviews are conducted in product development and deployment phases to mitigate potential risks.
  • Community Guidelines development and updates: Our Community Guidelines are developed and updated in response to emerging risks identified through external consultation, community feedback, and user reporting. This process includes conducting Human Rights Impact Assessments, an integral step to any changes or new developments to our Community Guidelines.
  • At-risk users: We recognize that certain users may face heightened risks such as human rights defenders and journalists. When we receive a credible report that a user is at risk of harm off-platform and that a third party may access their account without their permission, we may suspend or restrict access to the account to help protect the user and our community.
  • High-risk events: We have standard operating procedures to monitor and address high-risk events around the world, such as elections, civic unrest, conflicts, and humanitarian crises. Measures include (but are not limited to) enhancing risk monitoring capacity, in-app safety features and notifications, and being transparent about the measures and steps we take during these critical times.


Stakeholder engagement

We engage with human rights organizations, independent experts, members of affected communities, and our Advisory and Youth Councils to better understand emerging risks and inform our Trust and Safety approach. Their insights help shape our policies, product features, and safety processes. Through our Community Partner Channel, trusted safety and civil society organizations can directly report potentially violative content, providing valuable local expertise and context. We also collaborate with industry peers through a range of trust and safety, human rights, and child safety initiatives to stay informed of evolving risks and strengthen our collective response. For example, we are members of the WeProtect Global Alliance, Trust and Safety Professional Association (TSPA), Tech Against Terrorism, the Global Internet Forum to Counter Terrorism (GIFCT), Business for Social Responsibility, the Tech Coalition, Digital Trust & Safety Partnership, Tech Against Trafficking, and launch partners for Partnership on AI's Framework Responsible Practices for Synthetic Media.


Transparency

We believe transparency is essential to building trust and accountability, and we aim to provide clear information about our policies, processes, and the steps we take to keep our community safe.
